Why this chokepoint matters

In 2025, around 20 million barrels per day of crude oil and oil products passed through Hormuz. The IEA estimates this was about one-quarter of global seaborne oil trade.

A disruption at a narrow maritime corridor can affect buyers far beyond the conflict region through shipping, insurance, fuel and input costs.

How an oil shock spreads through the economy

A causal pathway to investigate. The size of each effect depends on duration, inventories, alternative routes, spare capacity and policy response.

01 · Supply

Conflict & infrastructure risk

Production interruption, shipping threats, route restrictions and uncertainty.

02 · Market

Price & freight volatility

Buyers seek alternatives; shipping, insurance and benchmark prices may rise.

03 · Industry

Higher input costs

Transport, aviation, manufacturing, petrochemicals and agriculture face cost pressure.

04 · Households

Inflation pressure

Fuel and goods may become more expensive, with uneven effects across countries.

Selected economic evidence

  • World Bank's October 2026 outlook forecast energy prices to rise 32% and overall commodity prices 23% in 2026.
  • The World Bank forecast Brent to average $91/barrel in 2026, compared with $69 in 2025. This is a forecast, not a final measured annual average.
  • UNCTAD discusses spillovers to trade, freight, insurance and financial conditions.

Resilience options

Policy and business responses that can reduce exposure; none is an instant or complete substitute for stable supply.

Diversify

  • Broaden supplier portfolios.
  • Assess alternative transport corridors.
  • Avoid excessive dependence on one chokepoint.

Prepare

  • Maintain appropriate emergency reserves.
  • Improve transparent market and shipping data.
  • Plan demand management and efficiency.

Transition

  • Expand reliable low-carbon electricity.
  • Improve public transport and energy efficiency.
  • Develop alternatives where suitable.

Protect people

  • Target support for vulnerable households.
  • Communicate price and supply risks clearly.
  • Track effects on food and essential services.
